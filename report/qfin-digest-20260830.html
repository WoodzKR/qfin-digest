The Tail Is the Only Signal: Flush Reversion in Equity Indices and Crypto Perpetual Futures

폭락 다음날 반등 효과는 대부분 집계 오류이고, 지수 -7% 이하만 살아남는다.

Almost all buy-the-dip reversion is a counting artifact; only index drops of 7%+ survive.

Alexandr Dashyan

  • 폭락 반등 백테스트가 같은 날짜를 중복 계산한다
  • 날짜 단위 집계, 중첩 임계값, 다중검정, 부트스트랩 보정
  • 지수 -7% 이하 50건만 다음날 평균 3.08% 수익
  • 암호화폐 무기한선물은 6년 내내 베타 차감 후 음수
방법
이벤트 스터디에 날짜 단위 집계와 familywise 보정, 짧은 블록 부트스트랩을 함께 적용한다.

미국 주가지수 4종의 76년치와 단일종목 28개 패널, 암호화폐 무기한선물 20종의 6년치를 쓴다.
시사점

진입·청산 규칙과 임계값이 명확하고 지수 일봉만 있으면 되므로 데이터 확보가 사실상 무료다.

  • Fix: date-level counting, nesting, familywise, bootstrap

Method
One event definition plus four corrections: date clustering, nested thresholds, familywise p-values, short-block bootstrap.
Data

Use
Buy the index at the close after a 7% or worse drop, exit next session, and size it as a rare overlay.
Score 5/5

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTBuying an asset immediately after a large single day decline is one of the oldest ideas in short horizon trading, and it is easy to make it look profitable. This paper applies one event definition and one testing protocol to two asset classes, four US equity indices over seventy six years and, in results carried over from an earlier stage of the same research program, twenty cryptocurrency perpetual futures, and finds that almost all of the apparent effect is an artifact of how the events are counted. Large declines arrive on the same few calendar dates across instruments, so an event study that treats each instrument day as an independent observation is counting one market wide shock many times. In a panel of twenty eight single names, 1,361 declines at or below seven percent are only 791 distinct dates, and the pooled t statistic of 6.76 falls to 1.68 when each date is counted once. Three further corrections matter and are usually skipped: the depth thresholds that define such events are nested rather than independent, so a strong tail leaks into every shallower cut; testing several depths requires a familywise correction; and a stationary block bootstrap with a block length that is a large fraction of the sample is badly anti conservative, which in our own first pass manufactured a p value two orders of magnitude too small. After all four corrections, exactly one result survives in equities. Index declines at or below seven percent, 50 events on 25 dates clustering into 10 episodes since 1950, are followed by a mean next session return of 3.08 percent, with a familywise p value of 0.006 and a bootstrap interval from 1.34 to 4.84 percent. It is not crash window drift: the next session returns 3.08 percent while each of the following nine averages 0.01 percent, below the unconditional daily mean. It is implementable, since 83 percent of the move occurs after the opening print. Everything shallower is indistinguishable from zero, and the corresponding single name effect is absent. In crypto perpetuals the same mechanism fails completely, with an edge net of market beta that is negative in every regime and every year of a six year sample. The economics are the final constraint: an account trading the surviving tail signal deploys 0.05 percent of its capital time and beats Treasury bills by 0.44 points a year, which is a real effect that cannot carry a portfolio.

The Omega Block: Correlation-Network Features that Improve Systemic Tail-Loss Attribution Beyond Downside Beta and Conditional MES

시사점
리스크 모델의 꼬리손실 예측 변수로 얹어 포트폴리오 집중도와 헤지 비중을 조정한다.
적용도 3/5

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTAttributing future systemic tail losses across assets remains dominated by exposures estimated asset by asset, market beta, downside beta and volatility, although drawdowns are precisely the episodes in which losses become collective and concentrate where shocks recirculate among tightly co-moving names. The quantities proposed here originate in our prior work on network fragility: the FSRI, a structural index for microbial co-occurrence networks in agricultural soils, and its financial descendant, the TSI, whose standalone version we falsified and reported. From that starting point we hypothesized that the same structural information could carry marginal value if localized to individual assets, extended with a response dimension, and handed to a flexible learner as features rather than composed by hand. The result is the Omega Block: five per-asset features computed from daily prices alone, two triadic quantities measured against a closed-form configuration null and three response quantities around scheduled FOMC announcements, among them Omega-R, a perturbation response normalized by each asset's own baseline drift, developed for this block. In pre-registered paired ablations with a same-width noise placebo, the block adds 1.3 to 3.4 points of out-of-fold R 2 to next-year tail-loss attribution over baselines that instantiate the public state of the art, up to 31 columns including sector, downside beta, co-moments, a conditional MES proxy, size, liquidity and balance-sheet fundamentals, replicated on two universes and two nearly disjoint periods (2012-2017, 2016-2025), while the placebo worsens the same model in every configuration. Because the baselines that decide adoption in practice are proprietary, the paper's verification is designed to travel to the data: an open acceptance harness reruns the entire protocol on any universe, baseline and event calendar, with a values-only mode and a cryptographic time-lock. The scope established by the evidence is cross-sectional attribution, not market timing.

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTWe examine whether NSE index derivatives exhibit a liquidity discontinuity at 15:00 IST, tied to mandatory broker RMS auto-square-off and, historically, the 15:00-15:30 IST closing-price VWAP window. ORIGINAL ABSTRACTThis study examines whether pre-expiration stock liquidity is associated with cross-sectional variation in price reactions to a potential increase in the supply of tradable shares, using IPO lockup expirations as pre-scheduled events. We construct a hand-collected dataset of lockup contracts from IPO disclosure documents for 522 IPO firms listed on the Mothers market of the Tokyo Stock Exchange between 2012 and 2021. Because lockup expiration dates are determined at the time of the IPO, this setting mitigates concerns that event timing is chosen in response to contemporaneous market conditions. We find a negative median cumulative abnormal return (CAR) around lockup expirations, and the magnitude of the reaction varies systematically with pre-expiration liquidity. Firms with higher pre-expiration turnover experience higher CAR, and this association remains significant in the joint specification. Higher Amihud illiquidity is associated with lower CAR in separate specifications, whereas wider bid-ask spreads show a marginally significant negative association; neither association remains statistically significant in the joint specification. The liquidity–CAR association is concentrated in the post-expiration window and is not statistically significant in the pre-expiration window, consistent with variation in post-expiration price reactions rather than pre-existing return trends. Overall, pre-expiration liquidity is associated with cross-sectional variation in post-expiration price reactions, with the most robust evidence coming from turnover.

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTWhen an AI system appears to generate equity alpha, five explanations compete with genuine skill: factor exposure, a weak benchmark, data leakage, model complexity, and an advantage a conventional system could replicate. We test all five on the same signal. Using the peer-reviewed replication of 212 published anomalies, language-model and agentic arms forecast which anomalies persist after their original samples end. The arms earn significant factor alpha-and none of it survives comparison with a passive portfolio of all published anomalies, which requires no model at all. Leakage does not explain the result: contamination made performance worse. The models' verbal mechanism judgements outperform their own numeric scores, but in-sample persistence absorbs them. A composite forecast scoring 0.400 owes 98.5% of that to a statistic we supplied. Apparent AI alpha here is a benchmarking artefact, not information.

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTSatellite-to-ground quantum key distribution (QKD) has been proposed and experimentally demonstrated (e.g., China's Micius satellite) as a means of extending quantum-secured communication beyond fiber-optic range, with direct relevance to naval fleet communications and aircraft-to-base communications where mobile assets cannot rely on fixed fiber infrastructure. Unlike this research group's companion terrestrial BB84 study, which evaluated eavesdropper detection under near-ideal channel conditions, satellite links are dominated by a different physical effect: atmospheric photon loss, driven by slant-path absorption, scattering, beam divergence, and elevation-angle-dependent atmospheric thickness. This study models that effect using PKTron's density-matrix simulator, explicitly separating two physically distinct phenomena that are frequently conflated in simplified models: heralded photon loss (which reduces raw key rate without directly causing bit errors) and detector dark counts (a roughly constant background click rate that becomes proportionally significant, and increasingly drives quantum bit error rate, QBER, as the real signal weakens). Across four simulated transmittance scenarios representing satellite pass geometries from zenith (η=0.50) to near-horizon (η=0.005), sifted key count fell from 547 to 15 bits per 2,000 transmitted qubits, while QBER rose from 2.6% to 33.3%, driven by dark counts constituting an increasing fraction (1.3% to 60.0%) of all detector clicks. Hardware validation was performed on IBM Quantum's ibm_marrakesh processor for the surviving-photon measurement step, using the same classical channel model to determine which photons survived transmission. At η=0.50, hardware measurement yielded a 0.550 click rate (matching theoretical prediction) with 12 sifted bits and 0.000 QBER; at η=0.03, a larger sample (400 transmitted qubits) yielded a 0.037 click rate (again matching prediction) with 8 sifted bits and a QBER of 0.125, consistent with the simulated prediction of elevated error rates at low transmittance. These results confirm, in both simulation and on real quantum hardware, that atmospheric loss degrades satellite QKD primarily through reduced key rate and a distinct, gradual, dark-count-driven QBER increase-qualitatively different from the sharp, immediate QBER signature of eavesdropping demonstrated in this group's companion terrestrial QKD study-a distinction with direct relevance to link budget engineering for naval and airborne quantum-secured communication systems.
