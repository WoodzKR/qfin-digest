ORIGINAL ABSTRACTMachine-learning volatility forecasts are usually judged by average out-of-sample accuracy, but their practical value depends on whether relative performance survives stress. This paper studies state-dependent forecast ranking rather than unconditional loss alone. Using the full 2,615-observation archived S&P 500 realized-volatility file preserved from the Oxford-Man Realized Library, I compare EWMA, HAR-RV, and XGBoost in a walk-forward design whose state thresholds use only information available through t-1. XGBoost outperforms HAR-RV in normal observations, but the ranking reverses under high volatility. The paired XGBoost-minus-HAR QLIKE differential is-0.0097 in normal states, +0.679 in Stress90, and +1.167 in Stress95; the Stress90-minus-Normal deterioration is +0.689. The pairwise reversal survives log-MSE and log-MAE, Holm adjustment, chronological subsamples, and a pre-specified top-5% one-day realizedvolatility-ratio state with 148 events. Moving-block bootstrap and leave-one-event-out diagnostics indicate that the jump-state result is not driven by any single observation, although the five largest events account for about 42% of the cumulative differential. By contrast, the primary Q50-to-Q90 transition definition identifies only eight events and remains descriptive under the pre-specified power gate. The evidence reveals a forecasting-risk paradox: a model can look competitive under unconditional average-error criteria while becoming materially worse when volatility is high or rising abruptly. The results motivate evaluating AI volatility forecasts by state-conditional ranking stability, not average accuracy alone.

ORIGINAL ABSTRACTHow do residential real estate markets value large-scale climate policies? We study how Germany's coal phase-out affects residential property values near decommissioned coal-fired power plants. Combining detailed residential property listing data from 2007 to 2023 with the staggered timing of plant closures, we estimate dynamic spatial difference-in-differences (DiD) models that account for the geographic proximity. Nearby residential property values decline by 8.2%, implying aggregate homeowner wealth losses of approximately €1.9 billion. To distinguish economic from environmental adjustment, we examine changes in local employment, population, purchasing power, and local air pollution. Declines in population, employment and purchasing power coincide with falling residential property values, whereas improvements in air quality do not seem to offset these losses. Our findings show that residential real estate markets primarily capitalize the deterioration of local economic fundamentals rather than improvements in environmental quality, implying that climate policy generates substantial localized household wealth effects through residential real estate markets - a distributional channel that complements conventional evaluations focused on aggregate environmental benefits and macroeconomic adjustment.
